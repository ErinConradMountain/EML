## Core EML Definition and Book Vision (from "What is EML")
- EML is a meta-language for thinking, learning, and teaching that makes learning visible, traceable, and revisable through relationships rather than facts.
- Three layers anchor the grammar: structural (nodes, links, flows, constraints, iterations), cognitive-temporal (repetition, delay, feedback, compression, drift), and agentic (learners, teachers, AI, notebooks, diagrams, classrooms as active agents).
- Pedagogy shifts from delivery to navigation: educator as cartographer, learner as model-builder, assessment as pattern recognition over time.
- The book is a model in book form: visual-first, calm, precise, iterative, with a left-page visual and right-page reflection rhythm.
- Four parts define the spine: Orientation, Grammar, Circuits in Practice, Futures.
- Visual language: black ink line drawings, hatching for depth, selective color (blue for structure, yellow for emergence, green for growth); diagrams recur with added resolution.
