## Faithful Summary

The exchange develops a central EML claim: a concept is not a fixed object held at one moment, but a path that becomes visible through movement, return, fading, and renewed framing. The discussion connects this to staged return: thoughts do not appear complete; they are re-encountered, stained by later contexts, and made more usable through repeated passage.

The strongest shift is from a layered diagram to a developmental system. A static diagram treats notes, concepts, and raw material as stacked objects. A developmental system treats them as phases in a movement: inscription, re-entry, pressure, mutation, and future trajectory.

The later part moves from object-centred study to field-centred study. A note is not merely a captured object; it draws a movement from latent space into a bounded field where the idea can be observed. Attention and framing create the field of study. Context then acts as terrain, gravity, or pressure, shaping how the idea moves and what it may become.

## Key Ideas

1. A concept is a path, not a thing.
2. Staged return is the method by which a concept becomes legible across time.
3. A note is an anchor of emergence, not only an archive item.
4. A field of study is created by selection and framing.
5. Context is not background; it is the terrain that gives the path shape.
