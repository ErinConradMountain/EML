Alignment literacy concerns the coordination of multiple components within a system: journal entries, graphical representations, and other informational artifacts. These components must move together toward the formation of a system that works in a systematic, structured, and conventional way. Convention here is not rigidity but shared grammar-something to be understood and learned. Each component part plays a role in the production of thought, and together they produce a meaning-making mechanism.

The handwritten journal entry is the initial site of representation. It serves as the point of entry for each cycle-typically a daily entry. From there, the handwritten draft undergoes a series of transformative steps. AI projections-this term feels appropriate in this context-participate in these transformations, moving the work toward clearer and more defined representations. The vision itself may remain abstract, but it must be housed within structure and material form. That structure provides usefulness through its particular commands and conventions-such as staging, commits, or pull requests in software grammar-which direct movement along a main branch.
