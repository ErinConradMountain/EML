Central study

The Read-Write Model

The Read-Write Model is the EML mechanism through which thought becomes visible, revisable, and trainable over time.

  1. Inscription: handwriting or another embodied mark anchors thought.
  2. Projection: AI or another tool returns structure, pattern, and possible form.
  3. Re-entry: the human reads the projection back into the body of thought.
  4. Revision: the human accepts, rejects, sharpens, or redirects the projection.
  5. Drift control: repeated return reveals whether the work is deepening or flattening.

How to use the site

Three useful pathways

EML is deliberately traceable: the public site points back to source documents, working studies, assets, and book drafts rather than hiding the route of the work.

For readers

Start with the book spine, then follow the Read-Write Model as the central mechanism of return.

For educators

Use the layers and circuits to notice how learners move through tools, marks, prompts, and classroom routines.

For contributors

Open the repository, read the project guide, and keep new material source-traceable before promotion.
