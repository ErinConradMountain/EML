## Working Thesis
Future casting is a grammatical act that formalizes anticipation without collapsing emergence, creating a bidirectional temporal grammar anchored in present inscription.

---

## Section Outline

1. **Writing as an IDE (Execution Environment)**
   - Frame writing as orchestration across platforms.
   - Define the present moment of inscription as the execution point.

2. **The Present Moment as Temporal Anchor**
   - Handwriting as constraint, not nostalgia.
   - Why anchor is required for both return and projection.

3. **Orientation Through Key Ideas**
   - Key ideas as configuration parameters, not content.
   - Governance function: bias attention and reduce drift.

4. **Visual Projection as Post-Clarity Transformation**
   - Infographics after meaning stabilizes.
   - Visuals as memory scaffolds and perceptual feedback.

5. **Near vs Distant Vision**
   - Tension between immediate orientation and long-range direction.
   - Why goals are insufficient; need a provisional grammar.

6. **Future Casting as Grammatical Act**
   - Definition: short predictive summary placed at the top of the page.
   - Rules: explicit, examinable, revisable; intent, not prediction.

7. **Bidirectional Temporality and Governance**
   - Return and anticipation as paired operations.
   - Oscillation as the learning rhythm.

8. **Practice Protocol (Minimal Version)**
   - One- or two-sentence cast per day.
   - 5-day beam visual to test alignment vs drift.
   - Weekly review: revise or discard casts.

9. **Implications for EML Grammar**
   - Anticipation becomes a teachable rule-set.
   - Governance shifts from output to rhythm management.
